# Voice

How the game speaks. Two registers, one tone. The tone is earnest drama (D12): poverty, crime, illness and death are treated seriously, with no comedy register. The two registers are second person in the interface and third-person past tense in the chronicle (D16). Every player-facing string follows this file; a voice pass over every string is a gate in Phase 2.

## The tone

- **The world responds; the text does not judge.** A theft is followed by what happens, not by an adjective. No moralising, no winking.
- **Facts carry the drama.** Names, times, amounts, what changed. If a line needs an adjective to land, the event behind it is too small.
- **Never glamorise.** Crime, drugs, violence and death are costs in the ledger and lines in the chronicle, not set pieces. No slow motion in words.
- **Respect the person.** Poverty, illness and addiction are written the way a careful friend would describe them: plainly, without pity and without jokes at the character's expense.
- **Plain words, short sentences.** No hype, no exclamation marks, no jargon from games ("buff", "grind", "RNG").
- **Numbers come from the rules.** Prices, times, odds and effects are formatted from the same values the sim uses. No number is typed into a string.

## Register 1: the interface, second person

Prompts, costs, choices, the HUD, settings and the last card address "you", present tense. "You" stays continuous across heirs; the person changes, the address does not.

Patterns:

- A choice shows its cost and its odds, flat: `Shoplift the bottle. About 1 in 6 someone notices. If they do, the store calls it in.`
- A state is a fact: `Rent is due Friday. You have $412.`
- A consequence is a notice, not a scold: `Marcy's let you go. Two no-shows in a month.`
- A need is a sensation, not a bar's name: `You have slept four hours in two days.`

Not this:

- `Uh oh! Rent's coming up!` (a joke, an exclamation mark)
- `Steal the goods?!` (a game asking you to be a character)
- `You feel sad.` (a meter speaking)
- `Hunger: 23%.` (a number the rules did not format, and a bar's name)

## Register 2: the chronicle, third person past

The journal records what happened, with names, in plain past tense, one or two sentences an event. It reads like a chronicle, not a diary: what was done and what followed, never what was felt unless someone said it.

Patterns:

- `Dana Kowalski worked the Friday dinner shift at Marcy's and was paid $94. She walked home in the rain and slept six hours.`
- `On 14 March the police came to the apartment with a warrant. Dana was held two nights and charged with theft under $500.`
- `Luis Ortega stopped answering her calls in April.`
- `Dana died on 3 November, aged 71, of heart failure, at St. Casimir's. Her son Michael was with her.`

Not this:

- `Dana's heart pounded as the cops burst in.` (interiority and adjectives doing the work)
- `Another rough day at Marcy's!` (a joke, no facts)
- `Luis is being distant.` (present tense, a judgement)

## Names and places

- People, businesses, streets and institutions are fictional. No real brands, chains, teams or public figures. The content lint keeps a blocklist.
- The city has its own names, drawn from the region's history: Polish, German, Irish, Black Southern, Appalachian and newer arrivals, in the proportions the generator gives a district.
- Institutions are named plainly: St. Casimir's, the Ninth Precinct, Marcy's, the county courthouse.

## What the voice forbids

- Melodrama, and adjectives that do the work of events.
- Jokes at the character's expense, and jokes about poverty, illness, addiction or death.
- Second-guessing the player ("Are you sure?") except where a real institution would ask.
- Glamour: no "epic", no "legendary", no heists written as fun.
- Game vocabulary in the fiction.
